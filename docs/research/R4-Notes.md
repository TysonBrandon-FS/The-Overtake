## F1 Merch Compliance Research

This research focused on whether it would be safe to use Formula 1 related images, especially photos from pro drivers Instagram accounts, on merch. The main thing I learned is that a picture being public online does not mean it is free to use on a shirt, hoodie, poster, or product. A driver photo could involve copyright from the photographer, publicity rights from the driver, and trademark issues if the image shows team logos, sponsor logos, or F1 branding.

I also learned that a disclaimer is helpful, but it does not give permission to use protected content. Saying the site is not official or not connected to Formula 1 can help avoid confusion, but it does not make it okay to use official logos, driver images, team names, or protected designs without permission. For merch, the safer direction is to make original racing inspired designs that do not copy official Formula 1 branding.

* Public Instagram photos are not automatically free to use on merch.
* Driver photos can involve copyright from the photographer and publicity rights from the driver.
* Official F1 logos, team logos, sponsor logos, and driver names can create trademark issues.
* A disclaimer can help explain the site is not official.
